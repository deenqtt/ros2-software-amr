# Stop slam_toolbox averaging away your ground truth

Your map is not drifting because slam_toolbox is badly tuned — it is drifting because Karto's matcher, in a noiseless featureless scene, finds many candidate poses that score *exactly* the same and then returns their arithmetic average. Reading the `ros2` branch source confirms this literally: after finding the best correlation response, `ScanMatcher::CorrelateScan` loops again over every candidate satisfying `math::DoubleEqual(response, bestResponse)`, accumulates positions and `cos/sin` of heading, and returns the centroid ([Mapper.cpp](https://github.com/SteveMacenski/slam_toolbox/blob/ros2/lib/karto_sdk/src/Mapper.cpp)). The only thing anchoring the degenerate axes to your near-perfect odometry is a multiplicative penalty with a hard-coded gain of `0.2` and a floor — and in your configuration that penalty is worth about **2.5% of response in translation and 0.08% in heading**, far too weak to break an exact numerical tie. Your `map->odom` yaw series (+5.199, −6.999, +2.151, +3.142, +8.947, +2.165 deg) flips sign and does not accumulate, which is the signature of per-run ambiguity resolution rather than dead-reckoning drift; Macenski's stated accuracy expectation for this package is **about 5 cm** ([slam_toolbox#239](https://github.com/SteveMacenski/slam_toolbox/issues/239)), and an 8.947 deg map yaw error displaces a wall by more than your 0.582 m robot width at only **3.7 m** from the pivot — which is a sufficient explanation for a map that split into two unplannable regions. Of your two edits, narrowing `coarse_search_angle_offset` points the right way but is actively dangerous while the default scan-admission gate lets whole turns through in one node, and raising `angle_variance_penalty` from 1.0 to 2.0 is **the wrong sign and numerically inert**. The fastest route to a usable Nav2 map is not tuning at all: Isaac Sim will raycast the stage into a ROS occupancy grid in minutes.

## Karto averages its ambiguity, so corridors compress rather than wander

The matcher does not minimise point-to-point distance. It maximises a normalised hit count in [0,1] — `response += pByte[pAngleIndexPointer[i]]` then `response /= (nPoints * GridStates_Occupied)` — over a brute-force sweep of `m_xPoses × m_yPoses × nAngles` candidate offsets, coarse pass then fine pass ([Mapper.cpp](https://github.com/SteveMacenski/slam_toolbox/blob/ros2/lib/karto_sdk/src/Mapper.cpp)). Sliding a scan along a corridor axis changes that hit count by nothing, so the response surface is a flat ridge, not a peak. Because ties are averaged, the returned pose collapses to the midpoint of the ambiguity — which, since the search is centred on the odometry prediction, systematically *understates* travel. This is why the classic symptom is a **short, compressed corridor with a duplicated wall on the return leg** rather than a random walk. The degeneracy taxonomy in the literature classes "(a) long corridor; (b) empty space" together as ambiguous environments causing perceptual aliasing of laser scans ([*Sensors* 19(15):3331](https://doi.org/10.3390/s19153331) — note the research notes flag this paper as only **partially verified**, since the full text returned HTTP 403), and 3D registration work describes the same geometry as unobservable components being "filled from the initial guess or noise" ([X-ICP, arXiv:2211.16335](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.16335)). A 2D-specific peer-reviewed statement is blunter: "Corridor scenes with two parallel featureless walls are very challenging for scan matching" ([arXiv:2106.14003](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.14003)).

Perfect simulated geometry makes this worse, and the mechanism is specific rather than mystical. The tie-set is selected by exact floating-point comparison, so noiseless ranges against a perfectly straight axis-aligned wall produce *numerically identical* hit counts across many offsets; real range noise and wall irregularity perturb those counts until one candidate wins outright and the average collapses to a single pose. The code even guards against the fully flat outcome — `ComputePositionalCovariance` carries the comment that values "might be 0 if points are too sparse and thus don't hit other points" and overrides them to `MAX_VARIANCE = 500.0` ([Mapper.cpp](https://github.com/SteveMacenski/slam_toolbox/blob/ros2/lib/karto_sdk/src/Mapper.cpp)). This is exactly Macenski's position, stated in a Webots case with accurate `/odom` and a fully drifting map: "its an artifact of the overwhelming 'perfectness' of the simulated environment paired with very few distinctive features to lock the system into a solution… that's an issue in simulation only" ([slam_toolbox#586](https://github.com/SteveMacenski/slam_toolbox/issues/586)). The research notes record that **no controlled published experiment** holds geometry fixed and toggles LiDAR noise to demonstrate this, so treat noise-sharpens-the-argmax as a source-grounded mechanism plus a maintainer claim, not a measured result.

Your instrument is the right one. `setTransformFromPoses` builds `map_to_odom_` as `(map->base from the pose graph) ∘ (odom->base from dead reckoning)⁻¹`, initialised to identity and republished continuously ([slam_toolbox_common.cpp](https://github.com/SteveMacenski/slam_toolbox/blob/ros2/src/slam_toolbox_common.cpp)). When odometry tracks ground truth, `map->odom` **is** SLAM's own error, and the usual real-world reading inverts: a growing `map->odom` in your simulator means SLAM is corrupting a correct estimate. One caveat from the maintainer — slam_toolbox consumes **TF, not the `/odom` topic** ("We don't use the `/odom` topic for odometry, we use TF" — [slam_toolbox#586](https://github.com/SteveMacenski/slam_toolbox/issues/586)) — so instrument `odom->base_link` from TF or you may be measuring a signal the matcher never saw.

Two more measurements are free and you are not yet using them. First, `/pose` (`PoseWithCovarianceStamped`) carries the per-match covariance at indices 0, 7 and 35, and Macenski confirms it is the intended confidence signal: "We have a covariance that is scaled by the variable scales which indicates confidence of the latest scan match" ([slam_toolbox#816](https://github.com/SteveMacenski/slam_toolbox/issues/816)). That covariance is literally a plateau-width measurement — `ComputePositionalCovariance` keeps every cell with `response >= bestResponse - 0.1` and accumulates response-weighted second moments, scaled by `1/bestResponse` ([Mapper.cpp](https://github.com/SteveMacenski/slam_toolbox/blob/ros2/lib/karto_sdk/src/Mapper.cpp)) — so a large `xx` with a small `yy` in a corridor-aligned frame is a *positive identification* of along-corridor degeneracy rather than generic drift, and values near 500.0 mean the matcher found no usable response at all. Second, watch the error's shape: the research notes' discriminator is that odometry drift is a **ramp proportional to distance**, while degenerate matching is a **staircase co-located with turns and corridor mouths**. Your six runs support the staircase reading, because the yaw reverses sign and the magnitude does not track run length. Note what that also implies about your team lead's technique change: 2.151 deg was already achieved *before* it, so a single 2.165 deg run sits inside the existing spread and does not yet demonstrate an effect. With scatter of ±9 deg, you cannot attribute anything to technique or parameters from live drives; you need a frozen input, which is the offline-bag argument below.

For calibrating "how bad", the record gives you anchors at both ends but nothing authoritative in between. Macenski's expectation is ~5 cm ([#239](https://github.com/SteveMacenski/slam_toolbox/issues/239)). User-reported failures cluster at 40 cm of pose-graph shift over a >100 m corridor route ([slam_toolbox#334](https://github.com/SteveMacenski/slam_toolbox/issues/334)) and ~1.8 m total localisation error in symmetric corridors, closed with no maintainer reply and therefore **unverified** ([slam_toolbox#769](https://github.com/SteveMacenski/slam_toolbox/issues/769)). There is **no maintainer-endorsed threshold for acceptable `map->odom` magnitude** — that number does not exist upstream.

### Why the map split in two, quantitatively

Take the yaw error as a rotation of one part of the graph relative to another and ask at what lever arm it exceeds your robot's 0.582 m width:

| `map->odom` yaw | Lateral wall displacement exceeds 0.582 m beyond |
|---|---|
| 2.151 deg | 15.5 m |
| 2.165 deg | 15.4 m |
| 3.142 deg | 10.6 m |
| 5.199 deg | 6.4 m |
| 6.999 deg | 4.7 m |
| 8.947 deg | **3.7 m** |

*(my calculation, `0.582 / tan(yaw)`)*. On your worst run, any wall more than 3.7 m from the rotation point is misplaced by more than a robot width — which is exactly how a genuinely contiguous corridor becomes two grid regions separated by a band of occupied or unknown cells that Nav2 cannot plan through. Even your best runs corrupt geometry beyond ~15 m. The per-match error is bounded by the search window, so this is accumulated rather than injected in one step: `correlation_search_space_dimension: 0.5` permits ±0.25 m and `coarse_search_angle_offset: 0.349` permits ±20° of displacement per node ([config/mapper_params_online_async.yaml](https://github.com/SteveMacenski/slam_toolbox/blob/ros2/config/mapper_params_online_async.yaml)).

## Your `angle_variance_penalty` edit points backwards and cannot bite anyway

Here is the exact penalty code, whose comment describes it as a "simple model (approximate Gaussian) to take odometry into account" ([Mapper.cpp](https://github.com/SteveMacenski/slam_toolbox/blob/ros2/lib/karto_sdk/src/Mapper.cpp)):

```cpp
#define DISTANCE_PENALTY_GAIN   0.2
#define ANGLE_PENALTY_GAIN      0.2
...
distancePenalty = 1.0 - (DISTANCE_PENALTY_GAIN * squaredDistance / distance_variance_penalty);
distancePenalty = max(distancePenalty, minimum_distance_penalty);
anglePenalty    = 1.0 - (ANGLE_PENALTY_GAIN * squaredAngleDistance / angle_variance_penalty);
anglePenalty    = max(anglePenalty, minimum_angle_penalty);
response *= (distancePenalty * anglePenalty);
```

The variance sits in the **denominator**. A *larger* `angle_variance_penalty` therefore produces a *weaker* penalty, trusts odometry *less*, and lets the matcher move further from the odometric heading. Raising 1.0 → 2.0 is the opposite of "lean harder on my ground-truth odometry"; if that was the intent, the source-consistent direction is **down** (toward ~0.3), and it must be paired with lowering `minimum_angle_penalty` from 0.9, because that clamp caps the entire angular penalty at a 10% response reduction no matter what the variance is. The research notes flag that this inverted advice is widespread, that the README states no direction, and that Macenski's 2019 phrasing ("the Penalty parameters will add a penalty for more distance or angle traversed to find a match, so you may want to **relax those**" — [slam_toolbox#140](https://github.com/SteveMacenski/slam_toolbox/issues/140)) probably seeded it. The one ROS 2 Jazzy practitioner who lowered both penalties to 0.1 with the stated rationale "Reduce odometry reliance, increase scan matching" had the direction inverted *and* reported it did not help ([Hackaday.io, Perceptor](https://hackaday.io/project/203547-perceptor-autonomous-floor-mapping-and-navigation/log/242263-initial-challenges-when-slam-goes-wrong)).

Worse for your case, the edit is numerically irrelevant. Inside your narrowed ±0.0873 rad window the largest achievable `squaredAngleDistance` is 0.00762 rad², so the angle penalty moves from **0.99848** (variance 1.0) to **0.99924** (variance 2.0) — a difference of 0.0008 in the response multiplier *(my calculation from the code above)*. Ties are detected by exact equality via `math::DoubleEqual`. A prior worth eight parts in ten thousand cannot break an exact numerical tie, so in your configuration the odometry angular prior does not participate in tie-breaking at all. The translational side is barely better: at the ±0.25 m window edge the distance penalty is **0.975**.

Your other edit, `coarse_search_angle_offset` 0.349 → 0.0873, points in the defensible direction — narrowing removes far-away angular local maxima, which is precisely the failure mode in near-symmetric scenes — but the research notes label the whole "window width vs odometry quality" rule as **community folklore with no citable primary source**; every ROS 2 config the researchers retrieved, including TurtleBot 4's, leaves all three angle parameters at default. And narrowing has a trap you are almost certainly hitting, which is the most actionable finding in these notes.

### The scan-admission gate, not the search window, is your real problem

There are two independent "moved enough" tests. Karto's `HasMovedEnough` is a pure OR over time, heading and distance, and its *first* check returns true unconditionally when `timeInterval >= minimum_time_interval` (0.5 s). But the ROS wrapper's `shouldProcessScan` runs first, and with the default `check_min_dist_and_heading_precisely: false` it tests **distance only**:

```cpp
} else if (dist2 < 0.8 * min_dist2) {
  // within 10% for correction error, min heading is occasionally checked in the mapper
  return false;
}
```

([slam_toolbox_common.cpp](https://github.com/SteveMacenski/slam_toolbox/blob/ros2/src/slam_toolbox_common.cpp)). During a pure in-place rotation, `dist2 ≈ 0`, so the wrapper drops every scan until roughly **0.45 m of translation** has accumulated — `minimum_travel_heading: 0.5` never gets consulted. PR #888 documents this explicitly: the pre-filter "never checked `minimum_travel_heading` … causing in-place rotations to never trigger scan processing regardless of how far the robot turned", and the wrapper compares raw `base_link` poses while karto compares *sensor* poses, "creating frame mismatches for robots with non-trivial laser offsets". The PR is **open and not merged** — Macenski rejected it over an unclean diff and noted backports must not change default behaviour ([slam_toolbox#888](https://github.com/SteveMacenski/slam_toolbox/pull/888)).

The consequence: when you rotate 90° in place and drive on, the next admitted node carries the *entire* accumulated heading change from its predecessor. That is 1.57 rad against a coarse window of ±0.349 rad by default, or ±0.0873 rad in your config. The response collapses, and `use_response_expansion: true` (the default) then *widens* the angular search by +20° up to three times, out to roughly ±80°, and whatever pose that badly-conditioned match returns is committed as a graph node in one discrete step ([Mapper.cpp](https://github.com/SteveMacenski/slam_toolbox/blob/ros2/lib/karto_sdk/src/Mapper.cpp)). This is the mechanism behind the maintainer-triaged report "when we rotate the robot, the map does not map new laserscans, after substantial rotations, it suddenly jumps and maps all the new laserscan points" ([slam_toolbox#698](https://github.com/SteveMacenski/slam_toolbox/issues/698), redirected by Macenski to the featureless-environment explanation in [#239](https://github.com/SteveMacenski/slam_toolbox/issues/239)). It also gives your team lead's slower driving a real mechanism rather than a superstitious one — but only in the wide-window default. Narrowing the window while the gate still admits whole turns in a single node is **the worst of both configurations**: you have removed the matcher's ability to bracket the true heading change without reducing the heading change it is asked to bracket.

Read that table's third column as the headline finding: **nobody in the retrieved record changes the correlation search space, the angle offsets, or the loop-closure thresholds.** TurtleBot 4's entire deviation from upstream is frames, topic, range, node density and buffer size — the scan-matcher internals and `ceres_loss_function: None` are untouched. That is the tuning discipline the evidence supports, and it is what Macenski means by de-tuning: "you've messed with the configs greatly and essentially de-tuned the system I worked reasonably hard to provide a reasonable out of the box baseline with — in which case reverting parameters and starting tuning again might be good" ([#586](https://github.com/SteveMacenski/slam_toolbox/issues/586)).

On loop closure, two source facts change how you should think about it. Ceres runs *only* on a closure — "everytime you see those `Solver Summary` Ceres prints, that is a loop closure process occurring… Ceres is only called when we've found a loop" ([slam_toolbox#609](https://github.com/SteveMacenski/slam_toolbox/issues/609)) — so no Ceres print means no closure has ever fired, and that is the first thing to check in your logs. And the dominant silent failure is chain starvation: in `FindPossibleLoopClosure`, any candidate scan already *linked* to the current scan calls `chain.clear()`, wiping the whole accumulating chain, and a run shorter than `loop_match_minimum_chain_size` is discarded the same way ([Mapper.cpp](https://github.com/SteveMacenski/slam_toolbox/blob/ros2/lib/karto_sdk/src/Mapper.cpp)). With `minimum_travel_distance: 0.5`, ten qualifying scans demand roughly **5 m of travel through the revisit region** — tapping the start point and stopping cannot close a loop. Loop closure is also not free: in #198 closures rotated the map 180° with a backwards-mounted RPLIDAR and "Disabling loop closure prevents the bug" ([slam_toolbox#198](https://github.com/SteveMacenski/slam_toolbox/issues/198)), which is why lowering the response thresholds is the highest-risk edit in the file. `ceres_loss_function` will not save you either; the maintainer's verdict is that "a change in loss function can do wonders for removing the symptoms of a problem (until it grows to a point it can't…) but its not a solution in it of itself for anything" ([slam_toolbox#334](https://github.com/SteveMacenski/slam_toolbox/issues/334)).

## Drive small closed loops, not slow straight lines

ROS 2's own ecosystem documents essentially no technique. Clearpath's entire guidance is "Drive the robot around the world" ([Clearpath SLAM tutorial](https://docs.clearpathrobotics.com/docs/ros/tutorials/navigation_demos/slam/)); Husarion's is "drive your robot around and observe as new parts of map are added" ([Husarion](https://husarion.com/tutorials/ros2-tutorials/8-slam/)). The widely circulated "max 0.15 m/s while mapping" number is flagged in the research notes as **folklore with no primary source** — it appears only in uncited robotics blogs, with no robot model and no derivation. The *direction* is supported, but by adjacent industries: handheld/trolley LiDAR mapping guidance specifies **0.5–1.0 m/s** generally and **≤0.5 m/s** in narrow spaces, corridors and tunnels, says to "slow down when turning, and avoid large device swings", and at corners to "use a larger turning radius and keep the scanner facing the inner corner wall" ([SHARE3DCAM](https://www.share3dcam.com/en/faqinfo2.html)). Note that this is an order of magnitude looser than the folklore number.

Two further popular rules also come out as folklore. "Rotate in place at corners to seed the map" has **no primary source either way**, and the sourced signals point the *opposite* direction — the handheld guidance says to avoid rapid rotation in place and prefer a wide radius, and in slam_toolbox's own code an in-place spin is precisely what the wrapper gate cannot admit. "Wall-following beats cutting across open space" likewise has **no primary source for 2D LiDAR SLAM**, and the peer-reviewed drift study points the other way: internal loops "measured toward the middle of the room" are what minimise drift ([Automation in Construction 2022](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0926580522005702)). That paper names your exact hotspots — drift is generated by "narrow staircases, long narrow hallways, narrow doorways, and visits from inside to outside" — and prescribes that "the measurement path should revisit the environments that have been measured".

What the sources do converge on, and what is worth writing into your survey SOP: small closed loops rather than long out-and-backs, overlap and **cross** your own previous path, box-shaped rather than sprawling routes so the path between successive closures stays short ([NavVis VLX best practices](https://vlx2.docs.navvis.com/operation/using-the-device/best-practices/loop-closures/) — retrieved by the researchers via search summary, so treat the wording as paraphrase), loops every 100–200 m with 5–20 m of overlap ([SHARE3DCAM](https://www.share3dcam.com/en/faqinfo2.html)), and at a corridor-to-room threshold, keep both spaces visible for several seconds before committing: "When two rooms are connected by a narrow doorway, slowly enter sideways… Keep both rooms visible for several seconds until the system establishes feature correspondence before fully entering" ([SHARE3DCAM](https://www.share3dcam.com/en/faqinfo2.html)). The popular "exit the corridor, drive straight until the room's walls are mapped, then turn" rule has **no primary source in those terms** — but the mechanism behind it is sound and worth stating as engineering reasoning: the corridor's accumulated error is longitudinal, the first scans that see the room's far wall are what fix it, and turning immediately adds rotation at the moment pose error is largest. One practical note in your favour: dwelling at a revisit point with small odometry changes is precisely the workaround a practitioner used to force closures to fire reliably ([slam_toolbox#609](https://github.com/SteveMacenski/slam_toolbox/issues/609)), and the same thread reports that repeated returns do **not** monotonically improve closure quality. Finally, because scan admission is threshold-gated, speed does not change *which* scans enter the graph — it changes each admitted scan's quality (motion skew, larger odometry extrapolation, larger pose error inside the search window). That is an inference from the parameter semantics, and it explains why your slower run produced a result statistically indistinguishable from earlier fast ones.

Commissioning practice also says a hand-driven first map is normal, not a shortcut. BlueBotics ANT, OEM'd into many AMRs, maps a hybrid forklift "by driving this hybrid vehicle as normal, using its steering wheel" and a fully autonomous vehicle with "a wired joystick", records everything the LiDARs see, and imports the result into ANT lab to be "transformed into a raw site map" containing both permanent fixtures and temporary objects ([ANT Answers](https://insights.antdriven.com/agv-amr-installation)). Vendors expect the raw map to need manual cleanup — so an imperfect Isaac map that you then edit is also normal practice.

## `use_scan_matching: false` silently deletes your pose graph

The verdict is no, and for a stronger reason than purity. In the current `ros2` branch, the graph itself lives inside the `use_scan_matching` guard:

```cpp
Vertex<LocalizedRangeScan> * scan_vertex = NULL;
if (m_pUseScanMatching->GetValue()) {
  scan_vertex = m_pGraph->AddVertex(pScan);
  m_pGraph->AddEdges(pScan, cov);
  m_pMapperSensorManager->AddRunningScan(pScan);
  if (m_pDoLoopClosing->GetValue()) { ... m_pGraph->TryCloseLoop(pScan, *iter); }
}
```

([Mapper.cpp](https://github.com/SteveMacenski/slam_toolbox/blob/ros2/lib/karto_sdk/src/Mapper.cpp), repeated in every `Process`/`ProcessAgainstNode` variant). So the popular "turn off scan matching but keep loop closure" configuration is **impossible in this code**: `do_loop_closing: true` becomes a no-op, no vertices or edges are created, serialisation yields a degenerate `.posegraph`, graph visualisation is empty, RViz interactive mode has nothing to drag, `mode: localization` has no graph to localise against, and the published covariance stays a meaningless identity. Each scan's corrected pose becomes the previous scan's odom→corrected transform applied to the new odometric pose — pure dead reckoning with no correction mechanism at all. The research notes label this consequence list as **their reading of the source**, not a maintainer enumeration; the closest official position is the README's parenthetical on `use_scan_matching`: "*(uh, why would you not?)*" ([README](https://github.com/SteveMacenski/slam_toolbox/blob/ros2/README.md)).

The issue most often cited as evidence for disabling it, #677, ends the other way. The reporter had 50 cm of drift over 240 m and wrote "With scan matching on the map is horrible" — then discovered his RPLIDAR A2M8 was a ~4.5–5 m variant, not the 12 m unit his `max_laser_range` claimed: "Switched to A2M12 and it was all gone. The map was perffffeecttttt with the default values" ([slam_toolbox#677](https://github.com/SteveMacenski/slam_toolbox/issues/677)). Anyone citing #677 for `use_scan_matching: false` is citing the first half of a thread that resolves against it — and the root cause was exactly the class of sensor-configuration bug that disabling scan matching hides. That is the check to run on your Isaac setup **before any tuning**: does `max_laser_range` match what the simulated LiDAR actually returns; are out-of-range returns published as `inf` or as finite ranges that the matcher will fit phantom walls to; is `use_sim_time` set on *every* node; does the `base_link`→laser static transform's yaw match the USD mounting; and is your `base_frame` consistent (upstream's offline config defaults to `base_footprint`). Add one Isaac-specific timing check: a simulator that outruns slam_toolbox starves TF2's buffers, and the maintainer's diagnosis in #609 was that queue backlog "obviously things start to degrade by no fault of SLAM Toolbox" — the reporter's double-wall artifacts disappeared once the processing queue stayed under ~10 messages ([slam_toolbox#609](https://github.com/SteveMacenski/slam_toolbox/issues/609)).

The defensible middle ground keeps the graph and leans on odometry through the documented knobs: shrink `correlation_search_space_dimension` toward 0.1–0.2, set `use_response_expansion: false` so a shrunken window stays shrunken, narrow the angle offsets, **lower** the variance penalties and their `minimum_*_penalty` floors (note the sign, per the previous section), and raise `link_match_minimum_response_fine` so weak matches are dropped rather than applied ([README](https://github.com/SteveMacenski/slam_toolbox/blob/ros2/README.md)). Do not reach for `position_covariance_scale` / `yaw_covariance_scale` to fix the map — they affect downstream consumers only, not slam_toolbox's internal weighting. Worth flagging explicitly: the research notes contain an internal contradiction here, with one note reading the README's "penalty to apply to a matched scan as it differs from the odometric pose" as "raise it to trust odometry more". **The source-code derivation wins** — the variance is a denominator — and this contradiction is exactly how the inverted community advice propagates. There is also an upstream escape hatch in flight: PR #877 `runtime_disable_scan_matching`, whose stated purpose is "to prevent SLAM from causing false pose updates in ambiguous corridors or curves with no features", lets you switch matching off in specific regions while mapping continues on odometry ([slam_toolbox#877](https://github.com/SteveMacenski/slam_toolbox/pull/877)) — a **contributor** PR, not maintainer-endorsed, but direct evidence the problem is considered real.

## Isaac's occupancy map generator beats tuning for the map artefact

If what you need this week is a map Nav2 can plan through, stop doing SLAM for it. Isaac Sim ships an Occupancy Map Generator (**Tools > Robotics > Occupancy Map**, extension `isaacsim.asset.gen.omap`, enabled by default) that raycasts the stage's physics collision geometry and exports a binary occupancy image plus a **"ROS Occupancy Map Parameters File" (YAML)** with the parameters ROS needs, including image rotation for coordinate alignment ([Isaac Sim Occupancy Map docs](https://docs.isaacsim.omniverse.nvidia.com/5.1.0/digital_twin/ext_isaacsim_asset_generator_occupancy_map.html)). NVIDIA's own Nav2 tutorial parameterises it for a Nova Carter with origin at 0,0,0 and Z bounds 0.1 to 0.62, chosen to bracket the scan plane ([Isaac Sim ROS 2 Navigation tutorial](https://docs.isaacsim.omniverse.nvidia.com/6.0.0/ros2_tutorials/tutorial_ros2_navigation.html)). Two documented gotchas: "All geometry must have Collisions Enabled to be detected by the Occupancy Map Generator" or it is silently absent, and the starting location cannot be occupied or mapping fails. The cost is honest — this map has zero sensor realism, no unobservable corners, no partially-seen shelf legs — so it unblocks Nav2 development and validates nothing about your SLAM pipeline. Second-best, and closer to your real pipeline, is **mapping with known poses**: add a ROS2 Publish Raw Transform Tree node publishing `world -> odom` (`childFrameId: odom`, `parentFrameId: world`), which NVIDIA documents as standing in for "transforms that a localization package would normally provide" ([Isaac Sim ROS 2 Transform Trees and Odometry](https://docs.isaacsim.omniverse.nvidia.com/latest/ros2_tutorials/tutorial_ros2_tf.html)). That keeps the real simulated LiDAR, so occlusion and unknown-space structure stay realistic.

If you want the SLAM pipeline itself validated, the highest-value move is **offline bag reprocessing**, and it is the one thing on this list that directly fixes your measurement problem. slam_toolbox ships `launch/offline_launch.py`, which loads `config/mapper_params_offline.yaml` and launches the **`sync_slam_toolbox_node`** — not the async node — with `throttle_scans: 1` so every scan is processed and `enable_interactive_mode: true` ([offline_launch.py](https://github.com/SteveMacenski/slam_toolbox/blob/ros2/launch/offline_launch.py); [mapper_params_offline.yaml](https://github.com/SteveMacenski/slam_toolbox/blob/ros2/config/mapper_params_offline.yaml)). Record `/scan`, `/tf`, `/tf_static` (plus `/clock` on sim time) during one careful pass, then sweep parameters against that frozen input. With ±9 deg of run-to-run scatter in your live drives, a fixed dataset is the only way any parameter change becomes attributable — and it removes a whole class of cause, since Isaac competes with slam_toolbox for the same GPU and CPU. The research notes flag that **no published before/after measurement** demonstrates offline reprocessing yielding a better map; the argument rests on the node choice and config, and there is no upstream narrative tutorial for the workflow.

For the split map specifically, slam_toolbox's RViz plugin is the intended repair tool: "By default interactive mode is off… When you want to move nodes, tick the interactive box, move what you want, and save changes to prompt a manual loop closure", and kinematic map merging loads "any number of posegraphs that will show up in RVIZ under `map_N`" to be positioned by hand and merged into a global map ([README, jazzy](https://github.com/SteveMacenski/slam_toolbox/blob/jazzy/README.md)). A rotated map is, in pose-graph terms, often a single bad constraint, and adding a correct one by hand is more direct than blind re-tuning. Upstream says graph manipulation "isn't recommended for production use", so treat it as an unblocker producing a usable artefact, not a pipeline. Multi-session mapping via serialise/deserialise is legitimate as a *coverage* tool — map the cafe in short low-drift chunks and stitch them — but note lifelong mode's own caveat that it "should be used to build a complete map then switch to the pose-graph deformation localization mode until node decay is implemented", and that a March 2021 ABI break to fix 360° and non-axially-mounted LiDAR handling means serialised files are version-sensitive. Treat the bag as the durable artefact and the graph as derived.

Adding scene features is Macenski's own prescription — "if you made a more realistic environment that's not just an empty room, it quickly improves in performance if it has a few unique items to latch onto", with AWS warehouse, AWS hospital and Willow Garage named as known-good worlds ([#586](https://github.com/SteveMacenski/slam_toolbox/issues/586)) — and Isaac's stock warehouse asset already ships "shelving and objects that can be placed on them" ([Isaac Sim Environment Assets](https://docs.isaacsim.omniverse.nvidia.com/5.1.0/assets/usd_assets_environments.html)). Two warnings the research notes are emphatic about. First, **there is no quantitative guidance anywhere** on clutter density, minimum features per scan, or corners per loop; Macenski offers only "a few unique items". Second, a cafe scene is an especially bad case if modelled as identical tables and chairs on a regular grid — periodic geometry recreates the ambiguity and can make loop closure *worse* than a bare room by producing confident-but-wrong matches. Features must be irregularly spaced, differently sized, and intersect the scan plane at the LiDAR's mounting height; and in Isaac specifically, props with collisions disabled are invisible to the raycast even though they render, so verify by displaying physics meshes. Whether to do this at all depends on your real site: adding clutter is fidelity-*positive* if the real building is cluttered, fidelity-*negative* if it is a bare warehouse aisle.

Seeding from CAD is real practice but not your first move. `cad2ros` converts DWG/DXF/PNG/PDF to occupancy grids with "vector-accurate rendering with layer filtering, automatic thresholding, and center-aligned origin" ([zlxuans/cad2ros](https://github.com/zlxuans/cad2ros) — the researchers confirmed its existence and feature set but **not** its maintenance status or Jazzy compatibility), and the manual route is thresholding a blueprint in OpenCV plus a two-point scale calibration, emitting `.pgm` and `.yaml` with `occupied_thresh: 0.65` and `free_thresh: 0.196` ([Automatic Addison](https://automaticaddison.com/how-to-create-a-map-for-ros-from-a-floor-plan-or-blueprint/)). For a *simulated* scene, the USD stage already is the blueprint and the omap generator is the tracer — the CAD route matters for the real building later. What you should adopt from this literature now is its evaluation method: comparing SLAM output to a to-scale reference via **IoU**, with the reference cardinally aligned ([arXiv:2504.19654](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.19654)). Aligning your SLAM map to the omap output converts "the map looks rotated" into two numbers, and the recovered yaw is itself diagnostic — a clean constant rotation points at a frame or extrinsics bug, while a spatially varying warp points at pose-graph drift.

Cartographer is last, as an experiment. The two ROS 2-era comparisons both favour slam_toolbox: an MDPI study running both in Gazebo and on hardware under Humble reports **ATE 0.13 m for slam_toolbox vs 0.21 m for Cartographer** and credits slam_toolbox's stability to "its reliance on externally fused odometry" ([*Electronics* 14(24):4822](https://doi.org/10.3390/electronics14244822)), and an independent preprint calls Cartographer "the least efficient solution… an additional limitation is the absence of active maintenance and full compatibility with ROS 2" ([Authorea 10.22541/au.175199254.49549720](https://www.authorea.com/doi/pdf/10.22541/au.175199254.49549720)). Both are flagged **unverified against full text** in the research notes — MDPI and Authorea returned HTTP 403, so the figures come from indexed snippets. Since slam_toolbox's reported edge is precisely its use of a good odometry prior, and yours is near ground truth, switching to Cartographer trades away your strongest input. Its one genuine attraction is the submap/global split — "the job of local SLAM is to generate good submaps and the job of global SLAM is to tie them most consistently together", with submap size directly tunable via `num_range_data` ([Cartographer algorithm walkthrough](https://google-cartographer-ros.readthedocs.io/en/latest/algo_walkthrough.html)) — which is a real knob for a badly-stitched map. Note also that Cartographer's docs claim its real-time correlative matcher "is robust in feature rich environments" and make **no claim** about feature-poor ones; the research notes found **no study isolating feature-poverty as a variable**, so "Cartographer is better in featureless scenes" is unsupported in either direction.

## Conclusion

The sharpest reframing this research produces is that your two edits are fighting each other, and neither addresses the binding constraint. Narrowing the coarse angular window presumes the matcher is being asked to correct small heading errors; the wrapper's distance-only admission gate guarantees it is being asked to correct *whole turns*, because `minimum_travel_heading` is never consulted with `check_min_dist_and_heading_precisely: false`. Raising `angle_variance_penalty` was meant to lean on your ground-truth odometry and instead leans away from it, by an amount too small to matter either way. Fix the gate first — `check_min_dist_and_heading_precisely: true` with `minimum_travel_heading` around 0.2–0.3 rad, `minimum_time_interval: 0.25`, `scan_buffer_size: 20`, everything else back to upstream — and only then decide whether the angular window needs narrowing. That set is close to the TurtleBot 4 vendor profile, which is the only ROS 2 configuration in the record with a deployment behind it.

The deeper point is about epistemics rather than parameters. With ±9 deg of yaw scatter across six live runs, you have no instrument capable of detecting a parameter's effect, which is why your team lead's technique change reads as inconclusive: 2.151 deg predates it. Record one careful pass as a bag, replay it through `offline_launch.py` with the sync node, and generate the Isaac omap as a ground-truth reference so map error becomes an IoU plus a recovered yaw angle rather than an impression. That converts the whole problem from taste into measurement — and it gives you the one experiment nobody in the literature has run, since the research notes found no controlled study toggling LiDAR noise with geometry held fixed. You are one `range_noise` parameter away from producing it, and if adding noise to your Isaac LiDAR measurably shrinks `map->odom`, you will have confirmed the tie-set-averaging mechanism directly in your own scene and simultaneously made the simulation a fairer rehearsal for the real building.
